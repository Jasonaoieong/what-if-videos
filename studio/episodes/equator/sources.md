# Sources — where on Earth do you weigh less?
- Sea-level gravity ≈ 9.780 m/s² at the equator, ≈ 9.832 m/s² at the poles; ~0.5% heavier at the poles — https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity_of_Earth (NASA fact sheet: 9.78 eq / 9.83 pole).
- 80 kg (scale reading at the pole) → 80·9.780/9.832 = 79.58 kg at the equator (≈ 420 g, stated "about 400 grams").
- Spin part: ω²a = (7.292×10⁻⁵ s⁻¹)²·6,378 km = 0.0339 m/s² ≈ 0.34% → 276 g for 80 kg, ≈ 65% ("⅔") of the 0.052 m/s² difference; remainder from Earth's oblate shape.
- Radii: equatorial 6,378.1 km, polar 6,356.8 km (21.3 km) — NASA NSSDC Earth/Jupiter fact sheet https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/jupiterfact.html
- Equatorial ground speed 1,674 km/h (2π·6,378 km / 23.93 h).
- Extremes (GGMplus, Hirt et al. 2013): lowest 9.7639 m/s² Nevado Huascarán summit, Peru; highest 9.8337 m/s² Arctic Ocean surface — New Scientist https://www.newscientist.com/article/1987831-gravity-map-reveals-earths-extremes/ ; UPI https://www.upi.com/Science_News/2013/08/20/New-map-reveals-Earths-gravity-not-the-same-around-the-globe/87071377032414/ . 80 kg ratio → 79.43 kg.
- Globe is stylised (not real geography); time not to scale.
