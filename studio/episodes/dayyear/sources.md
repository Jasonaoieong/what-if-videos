# Sources — what if one day lasted a whole year?
- Scenario: solar day (sunrise to sunrise) = 1 year (365 days, 8,766 h) → half a year of daylight, half of night at any spot; Earth would barely turn relative to the stars (no rotation, or two turns a year). Axial tilt ignored.
- Moon: a full Sun-to-Sun cycle takes about 29 Earth days ("about a month"); surface ≈ 127 °C (260 °F) in full Sun, ≈ −173 °C (−280 °F) in darkness — NASA Moon facts https://science.nasa.gov/moon/facts/
- North Pole: day and night are theoretically six months long each (one sunrise, one sunset a year) — Britannica "Midnight Sun" https://www.britannica.com/science/midnight-Sun
- Venus: sidereal rotation 5,832.6 h ≈ 243 days (retrograde); orbit 224.7 days — NASA NSSDC Venus fact sheet https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/venusfact.html
- "Bake / freeze" is qualitative; no Earth temperatures are claimed. Globe stylised, time compressed.
