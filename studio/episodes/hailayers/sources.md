# Sources — why hailstones have layers
- NOAA NSSL, Severe Weather 101: Hail Basics (modified 2024-12-02), https://www.nssl.noaa.gov/education/svrwx101/hail/ :
  - "If the water freezes instantaneously when colliding with the hailstone, cloudy ice will form as air bubbles will be trapped … if the water freezes slowly, the air bubbles can escape and the new ice will be clear."
  - "Hailstones can have layers of clear and cloudy ice if the hailstone encounters different temperature and liquid water content conditions … as it passes horizontally across or near an updraft. The layers, however, do not occur simply due to the hailstone going through up and down cycles."
  - "Hailstones also do not grow from being lofted to the top of the thunderstorm. At very high altitudes, the air is cold enough (below -40°F) that all liquid water will have frozen" (−40 °F = −40 °C).
  - Fall speeds: 2–4 in hail 44–72 mph; > 4 in can exceed 100 mph.
- Old explanation ("layers like an onion if they travel up and down in an updraft … count the layers"): NSSL hail FAQ, 2008 archive, https://webarchive.library.unt.edu/web/20080916055924/http:/www.nssl.noaa.gov/primer/hail/hail_basics.html
