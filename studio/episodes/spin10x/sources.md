# Sources — Earth spinning 10× faster
- Equatorial rotation speed today 465.1 m/s = 1,674 km/h → ×10 = 4,651 m/s ≈ 16,744 km/h.
- Day length: sidereal day 86,164 s / 10 = 8,616 s ≈ 143.6 min ≈ 2 h 24 min (solar day differs by < 1 s) → ≈ 10 sunrises per 24 h.
- Weight: centrifugal acceleration at the equator today ω²R = (7.2921e-5 s⁻¹)²·6,378 km = 0.0339 m/s²; gravitational ≈ 9.814 m/s², effective 9.780 m/s². At 10× ω: 9.814 − 100·0.0339 = 6.42 m/s² → 0.657 of today's weight (an 80 kg person reads ≈ 52 kg). At the poles the centrifugal term is zero (ignoring the change in Earth's shape).
- Centrifugal effect ∝ ω² (100×); equatorial bulge grows and ocean water migrates toward the equator (as in the `faster` episode).
- Coriolis parameter f = 2Ω sin φ is proportional to the rotation rate → 10×.
- Haumea: "completes one rotation every 4 hours … The fast spin distorts Haumea's shape, making this dwarf planet look like a football." — NASA Science, https://science.nasa.gov/dwarf-planets/haumea/
