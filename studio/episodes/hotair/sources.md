# Sources — falling from a hot-air balloon
- Typical altitude: "Most commercial passenger hot air balloon rides cruise between 1,000 and 9,000 feet above ground" — Rainbow Ryders, https://rainbowryders.com/about/blog/hot-air-balloon-max-altitude/ . Episode uses 1,000 m (3,281 ft), inside that range.
- No wind in the basket: "Since the balloon moves with the direction of the winds, the passengers feel absolutely no wind" — Wikipedia, Hot air ballooning.
- Safety: "Hot air ballooning has been recognized by Fédération Aéronautique Internationale (FAI) as the safest air sport in aviation, and fatalities in hot air balloon accidents are rare, according to statistics from the NTSB" — Wikipedia, Hot air ballooning. Narration softened to "one of the safest air sports".
- Sim `simulate(1000)` (80 kg, belly-to-earth, sea-level Vt 55 m/s, NASA Glenn atmosphere): 143 km/h at 5 s, 193 km/h at 10 s, 198 km/h at 12 s, impact 199 km/h after 21.7 s.
- From 10,000 m (fall episode sim): impact ≈ 200 km/h.
- Balloon model is generic (no brand markings).
