# Sources — what if Earth's oceans drained?
- Average ocean depth 3,682 m; deepest point Challenger Deep (Mariana Trench) 10,935 m — NOAA Ocean Exploration https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/ocean-fact/ocean-depth/
- Everest 8,849 m (official 2020 survey height) → fits inside Challenger Deep.
- Mid-ocean ridge: nearly 65,000 km, more than 90% underwater, a continuous range of underwater volcanoes — NOAA Ocean Exploration https://oceanexplorer.noaa.gov/ocean-fact/mid-ocean-ridge/
- Mauna Kea ≈ 10,205 m from its submarine base to its peak, 4,205 m above sea level — Guinness World Records https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/tallest-mountain (narration rounds to 10,200 m).
- 27.3% of the ocean floor mapped to modern standards (Seabed 2030, World Hydrography Day, 21 June 2025) — https://tnfsa.nippon-foundation.or.jp/news/2756701 ("less than a third").
- All Earth's water as one sphere: ≈ 860 mi (≈ 1,384 km) across, ≈ 1,386,000,000 km³ — USGS https://www.usgs.gov/media/files/all-water-earth-a-sphere-placed-over-a-dry-globe
- Globe, seafloor and ridges are stylised (not real bathymetry); draining time is not to scale.
