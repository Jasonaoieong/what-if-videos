# Sources — what if gravity were twice as strong (skydiver)
- Model: 80 kg, belly-to-earth, CdA calibrated to 55 m/s sea-level terminal velocity at normal g (same as fall episode; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_velocity). Motion integrated with g = 2 × 9.80665 m/s², NASA Glenn standard atmosphere, exit 4,000 m, stop at 1,500 m (parachute altitude chosen for the scenario).
- Sim g×2: 314 km/h at 8 s; top 327 km/h at 12 s; 31.2 s to 1,500 m (304 km/h there).
- Same jump at normal g: top 231 km/h; 44.1 s to 1,500 m → 12.9 s ("13 seconds") sooner.
- Terminal velocity ∝ √g (drag ∝ v²) → 327/231 = 1.41 ≈ √2 ("about 40% faster").
- Weight 80·9.81 = 785 N → 1,569 N. Steady descent under canopy: drag = weight, so the canopy must carry twice the load.
- Jupiter: mean gravity at 1 bar 25.92 m/s², 2.64× Earth (equatorial 23.12, 2.36×) — NASA NSSDC Jupiter fact sheet https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/planetary/factsheet/jupiterfact.html ("about two and a half times").
